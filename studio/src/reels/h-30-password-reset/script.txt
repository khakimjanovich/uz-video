Friday evening, in another city, a stranger puts a new SIM card into a new phone. This afternoon, the mobile operator gave him Aziz's number. He installs the mobile bank and taps Forgot password. Every SMS to Aziz's number now arrives on his phone. Is that enough to take the account? In lesson twenty-nine, a password change ended every session of the same or a lower rank. This lesson opens the other door to a new password: the reset, when the person has no session at all. A reset in the master app starts with the phone number. Then the platform runs the checks of a login from that phone, without the password. On a known phone, that is a one-time code through TAF. On a new phone, it is the new-device code through TAF, and then the account check. Watch the stranger. The TAF code arrives on his phone, because the SIM card really has Aziz's number. The verdict can even be green. But Aziz was at level two, so the account check asks for MyID on this new phone. The stranger's face is not Aziz's face, and the reset stops. MyID protects Aziz. But what about a customer who was never at level two? There is no face on file to compare. For that customer, a second guard does the work: time. When the person still has an active session somewhere, the reset does not change the password at once. It waits for the configured waiting period. The platform answers with the time when the reset will end. And it sends a notification, password reset requested, by push to every session and by SMS. The real owner sees it on the phone in his pocket. Now only two parties can cancel the waiting reset. One is a session of rank two: a master-app session with a green TAF check. The other is a bank operator, with a reason. A session of rank one gets the notification, but no cancel button. Why so strict? A cancel keeps the old password alive, so only the strongest check may do it. When the owner taps Cancel, the old password stays valid, and a notification says that the reset was cancelled. When nobody cancels, the waiting period ends. The new password becomes valid, and the phone gets a session. Every other session of the same or a lower rank ends, and the probation period starts. Then the notification: password changed. I think this waiting reset is the most important rule against a SIM swap. An SMS code alone must never take an account from a person who is still using it. Now the honest case. On Sunday, Aziz himself forgets his password, on his own trusted phone. The mobile bank sends a code through TAF, and the verdict is green. Then the mobile bank offers him a MyID check, right inside the reset. He passes the re-check. With a green verdict and MyID on this phone, the reset does not wait. Aziz types a new password, and types it a second time. That is the deal the master app offers. Prove the SIM with TAF, and prove the face with MyID, and you skip the wait. A customer who was never at level two can do the same, with a full MyID check and consent. Without MyID, the reset waits. And when the person has no active session at all, there is nobody to warn, so the reset does not wait either. Now the side apps. They have no TAF and no MyID, so their reset is weaker. A side app may reset a password with an SMS code from the bank's SMS service. But only for a customer with no master-app account, who was never at level two. Everyone else must reset in the mobile bank. The side-app reset is limited per day, then a cool-down follows. And the waiting rule still applies when the customer has an active session. Yes, a SIM swap is still a risk for a customer who uses only side apps. The bank accepts that risk, on purpose, and writes it down. Last, the trail. Each step of each reset writes an audit event and an antifraud event, and sends a notification to every other session and by SMS. Resets for each customer are counted, too. And a bank operator can cancel or approve a waiting reset, always with a reason. To recap: the master-app reset runs the checks of a login, without the password. An active session makes the reset wait, and only a rank-two session or an operator can cancel it. Green TAF and MyID together make it immediate. And side apps reset by SMS only for customers the master app has never seen. But go back to the stranger with Aziz's SIM card. He did not forget the password. He guessed it, and he tries a normal login on his new phone. Aziz's phone lights up: Approve, or This was not me. What happens after he taps This was not me? Next: this was not me.
