When a payment page says paid, has any money moved? Picture a corner shop with a tab book. You take your bread, and the shopkeeper writes a line in the book. The money changes hands at the end of the week. A card payment works the same way. The page says paid in two seconds, but no money has moved yet. Two banks take part. The shop's bank is the acquirer, and this course builds its system. The buyer's bank is the issuer: it gave the buyer the card. When the buyer pays, the acquirer asks the issuer, through the card network, whether the payment is OK. The issuer checks the card and the money on the account, and says yes or no, in seconds. That yes is a line in the tab book, not money. The money moves later, in one batch for many payments, and that batch is called settlement. The acquirer keeps its fee and pays the shop the rest. Lesson twenty-two works out the fee, and lesson thirty-two counts the day. Giving money back depends on timing. Before settlement, the line is simply crossed out, as if it never happened. No money has moved, so none has to move back. After settlement, the money has moved, so it has to be sent back, and that is a refund. Code that mixes the two loses money, or pays twice. So keep two pairs apart. Approved is not paid out. And cancelled is not refunded. Now find the tab book in the code. In the ecom repository, search the payment statuses for the words given back. Two statuses come back. The first is given back before settlement, as if it never happened, and its name is reversed. The second is given back after settlement, and its name is refunded. Approved is not paid out, and cancel is not refund: the tab book is in the code. Next: what you may keep about a card.
