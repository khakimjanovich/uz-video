When you press Pay on a website, what happens behind that button? Follow one payment. A buyer picks a toy on a shop's website and presses Pay. The shop's server asks the bank's payments product, ecom, to create a payment. ecom answers with a link to the bank's own payment page. The buyer lands on that page, which shows the shop, the amount and the order. The card form on that page belongs to a second, smaller product: the card zone, the only place a card number ever lives. The buyer types the card, and it goes straight into the card zone, never through ecom or the shop. The card zone locks the number away and gives ecom a token instead: a stand-in that names the card without revealing it. ecom asks the card zone to charge the card by its token. The card zone talks to the card processing centre, which asks the buyer's bank. A yes comes back in seconds. The buyer sees paid, and the shop's server is told. At the end of the day, ecom counts what the shop earned, keeps the bank's fee, and reports the payout. It also checks every payment against the processing centre's own record. Three kinds of people use the system. The buyer only ever sees one page. The shop's owner and staff see their payments and refunds in a portal. The bank's staff approve shops and set fees. So the system is two products. ecom is the shop's side: merchants, fees, terminals, payments, refunds, reports, disputes, and checking the books. The card zone is the locked room for card numbers. They share nothing: each has its own database, its own keys and its own logins. This course builds both, one piece at a time: first the locked room, then the shop.
