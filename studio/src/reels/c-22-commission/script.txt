Who decides the bank's fee on a payment? In a supermarket, the tag on the item beats the sign on the aisle, which beats the offer for the whole store. The most specific price wins. The bank's fee works the same way. A fee rule can apply to one website, one merchant, one card, or the whole bank, and the most specific one wins. The fee is a percent of the amount, plus a fixed part, kept between a minimum and a maximum. Money is counted in whole cents, never in fractions, so the percent is rounded once. Rules are never edited in place. A change is proposed, and a second admin approves it. Two rules for the same thing can never apply at the same moment. And every payment keeps the rule it used, its fee, and what the merchant receives, so last month's payments can always be explained. Next: the virtual card machine each payment goes through.
