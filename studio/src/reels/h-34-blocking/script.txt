A Friday, months after the money phone. Aziz is at the bazaar, and the bazaar is loud. He reaches for his phone, and his pocket is empty. The phone is gone, and the mobile bank is on it. From his sister's phone, he calls the bank and says one thing: lock everything. Who in this system is allowed to lock Aziz out of his own account? Lesson eighteen ended with normal days: Aziz logs in on the same phone, with his password and a code. Lesson fifteen sent one kind of question to a person at the bank: the phone review. This lesson opens the panel where those people work, and shows the four things that they can do there. Start with the people. Bank staff sign in to the panel only through the bank's own staff identity, with a second factor there. The platform keeps no staff passwords, so there is no staff password list in it to steal. A staff session ends after a quiet time, and after a fixed lifetime, and the bank sets both. Each staff user has exactly one role, and only the operator role acts on customers. The platform checks the role at each action on the server, not only on the screen. An operator finds a customer only with an exact phone number, never with a name. In this panel, an operator has exactly four actions. First, the phone review from lesson fifteen: approve it, or reject it. An approval gives the customer level two. A rejection closes the customer at level one at once, and an SMS goes to the number. Second, the block. Third, the unblock. Fourth, a reset of the attempt counters, or of the mark that stops new MyID checks. Each action needs a written reason, and each action writes an audit event with the identity of the operator. Back to Friday. The operator types Aziz's number, finds him, and presses block, with the reason: phone lost, customer request. The block starts at once, and one operator is enough. Now watch what one block does. Every access grant of Aziz ends at its next request, as lesson seventeen showed. So the lost phone cannot move money, and it cannot even show a balance. Every request from his devices is refused. A login with his number is refused too, even with the right password. An open MyID check stops. But nothing is deleted: his devices and his verified identity stay as they are. A block is a pause, not an end. The platform sends the message account blocked, as an SMS to his number and as a push to his devices. It also writes an audit event and tells the antifraud system of the bank. A block is not the password lock of lesson eleven. That lock ends by itself after a time, but a block ends only when the operators lift it. So the answer to the first question is short: only an operator, with a reason. The platform never blocks a customer by itself. On Saturday, a stranger finds the phone in the bazaar. On Sunday, the stranger hands it in at the bazaar office, and Aziz has his phone again. He calls the bank one more time. The first operator asks for an unblock, with a reason. Nothing happens yet: the request waits as a proposal. A second operator opens it, reads the reason, and approves it. The operator who asked can never approve his own request. This rule has a name: maker-checker. One person makes the request, and a different person checks it. Only now does the unblock start. Each device gets back its state from before the block. His phone is the trusted phone again, the money phone, with no new MyID check. The platform sends the message account unblocked, and Aziz opens the mobile bank. The same two-person rule covers the approval of a phone review, and the reset. A rejection of a phone review gives nothing back, so it needs no second operator. Now a question for you: why does a block need one operator, but an unblock needs two? Because a block only takes access away and must be fast, but an unblock gives access back, and one tired or tricked person must not do that alone. Staff come in through the bank's own staff identity, with a second factor and one role each. An operator has four actions: the phone review, the block, the unblock and the reset. One operator blocks at once, and each action that gives access back needs a second operator. Every action has a reason and an audit event. The stranger held the phone for a night, and the push account blocked was on its screen. Lesson twenty shows what that push says, and why it says almost nothing about Aziz.
