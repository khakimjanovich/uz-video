A payment just ended: how does the shop's server find out, without asking again and again? If the payment were saved first and the news published after it, a crash in between would lose the news forever, or announce a payment that never committed. So the news goes into an outbox table, in the same transaction as the final status: they commit together, or not at all. The payment raises the event itself, whenever it becomes paid, declined, failed, or sent to manual review. A listener writes it to the outbox while the payment is saved, inside that same transaction. A relay runs every second, takes the oldest unpublished rows, and locks them with skip locked, so two instances never wait on each other or send the same row. It publishes each one to Kafka, keyed by the payment, so one payment's events stay in order, and marks it published only after Kafka has it. A crash in between sends it again, so every event carries its own id, and consumers drop repeats. The merchant sets one webhook address per website: HTTPS, on the website's own domain. A Kafka listener posts the event there, signed the Standard Webhooks way with the merchant's key: an event id, a timestamp, and an HMAC signature that any Standard Webhooks library can check. Anything but a two hundred is retried: one second, then two, then four, up to thirty, five times. After that, the event is parked on a dead-letter topic for a person. Every attempt is logged: where it went, and what the merchant answered. Postgres stays the truth, and Kafka only carries the news. Next, taking payments with no website at all: payment links.
