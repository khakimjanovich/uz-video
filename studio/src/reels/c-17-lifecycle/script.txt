Monday morning, Malika's shop exists, but it cannot take a single payment. In lesson sixteen, her checked email created the merchant and made her its first admin, and this lesson is how that merchant earns the right to take money, one checked document at a time. She opens Documents. The page says "Company documents", and explains that the bank checks who it works with before switching a merchant on. Two papers are marked Required: the company charter and the director's passport. Two more say "If it applies": a licence and a power of attorney. The bank must know who it does business with. That is know your business. A company that signs up in the morning and takes payments by lunch, with nobody having seen its papers, is the one that piles up complaints and disputed payments. And then nobody can say who it was, or who let it in. Malika clicks Choose file, picks her charter, and clicks Upload. The page says "Uploaded. The bank will review it." The status reads "Waiting for review". Each file goes into the file store. The store reads each file's real type from the file's own contents, never from its name. Someone else tries a trick: a file whose name says it is a document, but whose contents are something else. The picker lets it through, because it only looks at the name. The server reads the contents and refuses it. The store also keeps a checksum: a fingerprint worked out from every byte of the file. When the file is read back, its fingerprint must still match, so a document swapped after it was checked is caught. At the bank, Dilnoza opens the admin portal. She opens Merchants and picks Malika's shop. Each document has two buttons, Verify and Reject. Reject asks her to say what is wrong, so the merchant can read it and fix it. She verifies both. Only when every required document is verified can the company itself be verified. If Malika had not confirmed her email, the "Approve documents" button would stay grey, with a message saying so. Her email is confirmed, so Dilnoza clicks Approve documents. Then Sign contract, and she types the contract number, as printed on the signed paper. That number is unique: no two merchants ever share one. Last, she opens History. Every move is there: from where, to where, who, and why. That is the status history. I think it is the part most teams add last, and it should come first. It is written in the same step as the move itself, so a move without its record cannot happen. Months later, when someone asks why this shop was let in, the answer is on one screen: Dilnoza, on Monday, after both papers were verified. Know your business protects the bank, the card network and every buyer. The history protects Dilnoza too, because it shows exactly what she saw and when. Malika's shop now has checked papers and a contract number. It still cannot take money. Next: why switching it on needs two people.
