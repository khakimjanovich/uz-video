Where does a card number live, when the payments product must never see one? Everything that touches a card number is in P C I D S S scope, the strictest audit in payments. So all of it lives in one unit, cards: the card vault, the card frame, and the Uzcard and Humo adapters. ecom, the payments product, holds only card tokens and masked numbers, so ecom and any future caller stay out of that scope. The two units share nothing: each has its own database, its own Vault, its own Keycloak and its own monitoring. Cards never knows its callers: a caller is only a name in cards' inputs, plus a client certificate that cards' own certificate authority signed. Another caller, a mobile backend for example, changes cards' inputs, never its code or its deployment. ecom knows cards only by inputs too: the cards URLs, cards' certificate authority, its own client certificate, and one secret. Every call between services is mutual T L S, and it carries a token from cards' own Keycloak with the role the operation needs. Cards has two doors, and each lets through only what it must. The payer door serves only the card frame: the payer's browser loads it and posts the card, and every other path answers four oh four. Think of a T L S connection as a sealed envelope: S N I is the address written on it, and a certificate is the key that opens it. The partner door reads only the address, passes the envelope on unopened to the card vault, an adapter or Keycloak, and refuses any address it doesn't know. ecom gets its certificate the way a person gets a passport. ecom fills in the application, a C S R, with its own private key, which never leaves ecom. Cards, the passport office, stamps it with its seal and hands back three things: the certificate, cards' own certificate authority to recognise the real cards, and one secret for cards' Keycloak. Each allowed caller has its own roles, its own origins whose pages may embed the frame, and its own session limits. Fraud limits count refused cards and codes per card, per BIN, per payer address and per risk reference, each within the configured limit and window. Card numbers stay in one unit, behind two narrow doors, and every caller is only an input.
