Everything runs on one laptop: what does production need instead? Kubernetes runs each deployable as replicas behind a Service: the app three times, each adapter twice. Every image is the same Gradle-built jar on the plain JRE, as a user without root rights; the limits and JVM options come from the cluster. Spring Boot's own probes tell the cluster when a pod is alive, and when it takes traffic. Readiness counts only what the app cannot work without, the file store, Vault and Keycloak, so one card scheme down never takes the app out of rotation. A rolling update starts a new pod before it stops an old one, and a short pause before shutdown lets the Service stop sending requests first. The app then finishes what it is doing, for up to sixty seconds, longer than the slowest scheme call. Addresses live in a ConfigMap; secrets are read from Vault at startup, and the only Secret holds what signs in to Vault. Three replicas run every scheduled job three times, and that is safe: skip locked, one workflow per payment, ShedLock, and Kafka shares its partitions between them. An autoscaler adds replicas when the CPU is busy, and a disruption budget never lets a drain take more than one. Deleting a pod in the middle of payments loses nothing: the others take the traffic, and a new one starts. On a laptop, the same manifests run in a one-node k3s, with Services that point at the compose stack. Next, the last step: moving the card vault into its own service.
