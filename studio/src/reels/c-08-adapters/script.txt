How does one system talk to card networks that each speak a different language? Abroad, you carry a plug adapter. Every country's socket is different, but your laptop's plug never changes. Each card processing centre has its own protocol, its own error codes and its own way of saying yes. So the card zone has one adapter per centre: a small service that speaks the centre's language on one side, and one plain language on the other. Inside the card zone there is one plug shape for every centre. Charge, refund, check status: the same questions, whichever centre answers. And every answer becomes one of three outcomes: approved, declined, or unknown. A timeout is unknown, never declined, because the money may already have moved. So every charge carries our own payment id, and later we can ask the centre exactly what happened to it. A new centre means a new adapter, and nothing else changes.
