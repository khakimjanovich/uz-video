How does the bank know a request was really written by the merchant? A token says who you are, not that you wrote this request. So the merchant's server seals every request, like a letter with a wax seal, a date, and a serial number. The seal covers the method, the full address, the token, and the body, and it is made with a signing key only the bank and the merchant hold. The bank checks that the date is fresh, that the body matches the seal, and that the seal is right. Then it spends the serial number, so the same sealed request, recorded and sent again tomorrow, is refused. A changed amount breaks the seal. An old request is too late.
