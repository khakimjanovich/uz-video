Your bank holds Aziza's account, and she sells shoes online. But when her customers pay by card, the money goes to Payme and Click, not to you. With its own acquiring, your bank earns a commission on every payment. The rest stays in your bank too, as deposits. But what if a shell company with a fake director tries to get in? Before any store goes live, two bank employees check its documents and the sanctions lists. A website selling iPhones for fifty thousand soum can look completely real. A fraudulent or illegal website can't take payments through your bank. And what if an approved store suddenly starts sending dozens of suspicious payments? If anything slips through, your bank shuts it down in seconds. A hacker copies a real payment request and changes the amount to ten million. Every payment request is signed with the store's secret key, and works only once. A network glitch makes the store send the same payment twice, and the customer's phone buzzes. If a request is sent twice, the customer is still charged once. Now the store check goes dark, and a suspended store tries to sneak a payment through. When in doubt, your bank says no.
