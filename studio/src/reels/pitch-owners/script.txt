Your bank holds Aziza's account, and she sells shoes online. But when her customers pay by card, the money goes to Payme and Click, not to you. With its own acquiring, your bank earns a commission on every payment. The rest stays in your bank too, as deposits. But running payments means facing fraudsters. Here are the four threats every bank fears: fake shops, forged payments, double charges and stolen cards. Threat one, fake shops: next to Aziza's real shop, a site selling iPhones for fifty thousand soum looks just as real. Two bank employees check every shop, so only Aziza's real shop can take payments. Threat two, forged payments: a hacker copies Aziza's real payment and changes the amount to ten million. Every request is signed with Aziza's secret key, so the forged copy bounces off. Threat three, double charges: a network glitch makes her shop send the same payment twice, and her customer could be charged twice. Your bank charges the customer only once, and Aziza keeps a happy customer. Threat four, stolen cards: what if hackers break into Aziza's shop to steal her customers' card numbers? But cards are typed only on your bank's own page, and Aziza's shop keeps just a meaningless code, so there is nothing to steal. Every day, hundreds of payments like Aziza's land in your bank. Your bank keeps the commission, and each store gets the rest by the next banking day, in its account at your bank. To sum up: with its own acquiring, your bank earns a commission on every payment and keeps the deposits. And fake shops, forged payments, double charges and stolen cards all stop at your bank's door.
