On Saturday morning, Aziz opens the mobile bank to send money to his mother. He types the amount and taps Send. The money does not move, and no error appears. Instead, a screen full of text opens. So why does the bank show him text, and not the transfer? Aziz became a customer last Sunday. He has a number that TAF checked, a code that came to this phone, and a password that nobody can read. All of that is level one: the bank knows his phone, but not yet the person. Today he starts the way to level two, and this screen is the first step. Each app of the bank has a line in the installation: a name, a kind and a required level. The required level is the lowest level that the app asks for. The mobile bank has required level one, so Aziz used it right after registration. The app's own data needs only level one. But money needs level two, and so does data from the verified identity, like the verified address, cards or accounts. A transfer is money, so the transfer asks for level two. The easy answer is a red error: not allowed. The platform never gives that answer. Instead, the app asks for a step-up: the way from the level that Aziz has to the level that the action needs. The answer has three parts. The current level: one. The required level: two. And the checks that give level two: consent, a MyID check and a phone check, all in the mobile bank. Only the MyID check gives level two. No app, no operator and no setting can give it. So the app does not say no; it shows Aziz the way up. The first check is this screen of text: the consent gate. It stands before every call to MyID, and nothing reaches MyID around it. The screen has four fixed parts. The first part is the data that MyID reads from the state records: his names, his date and place of birth, his personal identification number, gender, citizenship, nationality, document data, registered addresses and phone number. The second part is the provider: MyID. The third part is the purpose: to identify him as a customer of this bank. The fourth part says who can read the data later: the bank's channels and its operators. It also says that TAF gets one thing, his personal identification number, for two checks after MyID. The bank writes the words of each part, but it cannot remove a part. The words come in versions. Each consent text and each terms text exists in the four languages of the installation: Uzbek Latin, Uzbek Cyrillic, Russian and English. Each version has a publication date, the staff user who proposed it, and a second staff user who approved it. The product ships a default text, compliance can change it, and the platform keeps every version. The screen always shows the version that is published at that moment, in the language of Aziz's journey. Aziz reads the fourth part twice, and taps Refuse. The platform opens no session at MyID. It counts no attempt, and Aziz stays at level one. It writes down only one thing: he refused. The money for his mother waits, but saying no costs Aziz nothing. That keeps the choice honest. In the afternoon, his mother calls: the rent is due on Monday. Aziz taps Send again, and the screen comes back. Nothing on it is selected in advance. His first answer is not copied, and no earlier yes ever counts. This time he reads to the end and taps Accept. Before any other step, the platform writes a record of his answer. It holds the versions of the consent text and the terms text, the language, the date and time, the device, the purpose, and Aziz. Only then may the platform open a session at MyID. One yes opens one session, no more. The record names the session that it allowed, so nobody can use the same yes twice. There is one more case. Suppose Aziz opens the screen and reads slowly. Meanwhile, compliance publishes a new version with a changed purpose. Aziz taps Accept on the old text. The platform refuses that answer and shows him the new text. The record must name exactly the version that he saw, word for word. On the map, the consent gate lives in the customer gateway, and the records of each answer live in Postgres. The mobile bank only shows the screen and sends the answer. Now a check for you. Aziz taps Refuse: how many MyID attempts does the platform count? None, because a refusal opens nothing at MyID. Each app has a required level, and money needs level two. When the level is too low, the platform does not refuse: the step-up names the level and the checks. The consent gate comes first, with four fixed parts and the published version. One yes opens one session, and a no costs nothing. Aziz has said yes, and the transfer to his mother waits for MyID. Lesson thirteen opens the MyID check itself.
