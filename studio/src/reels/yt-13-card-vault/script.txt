How do we know whether a card is Uzcard or Humo, and where do we keep its number? A card's first digits, its BIN, name the scheme. Both schemes publish their BIN lists, so the card module syncs them through the adapters into a registry, at startup and every night. The sync is idempotent, and a scheme that doesn't answer, or answers with nothing, keeps its old list. The longest matching prefix wins, so a bank's eight digit range beats the scheme's six digit one. Before any of that, the Luhn check digit catches a mistyped number. A plain card number in our database would be a disaster on the day of a breach. So the crypto platform library seals the number with AES two fifty six GCM: a fresh nonce every time, and the key id is authenticated with it. Each value names its key, so a new key can take over while older data still reads. Other modules get a card token instead, and the same card always gets the same token. To find a card without decrypting anything, we also store a keyed hash of its number, a blind index. The database itself refuses a card number stored in plain text. A number is decrypted only to hand it to the card's scheme, and every use is written to an audit trail. The bank's console shows the registry and finds a card by its number, without ever showing that number. Next, the payment itself, starting with a request nobody can forge.
