Can a payments product be P C I D S S compliant on its own? Picture a bank that buys a product whose brochure says compliant. The assessor asks for the firewall rules, the login logs, the people who hold the keys, and the camera footage of the server room. The vendor has none of them, because those belong to the bank's whole building, not to one room. Worse, nobody wrote down which jobs are the product's and which are the bank's, so some fall between. Lesson four gave the answer: we deliver the locked room, and the bank owns the building. So each unit ships a guide. It says what the bank's building must provide, how to install the room, and who does what. Who does what is a responsibility matrix. For each of the twelve requirements in P C I D S S, it lists what the product does, what is the bank's, and what is shared. A product row says what the code does; it is not a claim of compliance. Take the network: the product opens only two doors, the bank builds the firewalls, and the list of allowed connections is shared. Take logging: the product keeps audit trails, and collecting and reviewing them every day is the bank's job. ecom has its own matrix, because it holds no card number, and gets the smaller check from lesson four. Then come the known gaps: where the product still falls short at this release. Each gap names what the bank does until it is fixed. The product is not certified. The assessor is the person who judges the whole building, with the guide as the product's evidence. Now read the guide. Its first lines say the zone is the bank's, and that the guide claims no compliance. Section eleven is the matrix: product, bank and shared, row by row. Physical security is all the bank's, and the dual control of keys is shared. Section twelve lists the known gaps, each with the bank's control until it is fixed. And the guide ships with every release. We deliver the locked room; the bank owns the building. That is the whole course: the shop, the locked room, and the building around them both.
