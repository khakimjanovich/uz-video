Can a payments product be secure on its own? Picture a bank that buys a product whose brochure says compliant. The assessor asks for the firewall rules, the login logs, the people who hold the keys, and the cameras on the server room. The vendor has none of them, because those belong to the bank's whole building, not to one room. We deliver the locked room; the bank owns the building around it. So each product ships a guide: what the building must provide, how to install the room, and who does what. For each of the twelve requirements of PCI DSS, it says what the product does, what is the bank's, and what is shared. The network: the product opens only two doors, and the bank builds the firewalls. The keys: the product needs three of five keyholders, and the bank chooses them. The guide also lists, honestly, where the product still falls short, and what the bank does until it is fixed. The product is not certified on its own: the assessor judges the whole building. We deliver the locked room; the bank owns the building. That is the whole course: the shop, the locked room, and the building around them both.
