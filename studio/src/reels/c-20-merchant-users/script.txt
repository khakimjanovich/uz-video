The first week of March is the busiest week of Malika's year. Everyone buys flowers for the eighth, and she cannot answer every buyer alone. So she hires a helper for the week, and her accountant wants to see the payments too. In lesson sixteen, Malika became her shop's first admin, and in lesson nineteen its server got a login of its own, and this lesson gives the rest of her team their own logins and roles. The easy way is to share one login. I think it is the most common shortcut in small shops, and the most expensive one. If the helper sends a refund to a friend, the record says only that the shop did it. And when someone leaves, the password changes for everyone. So Malika opens Users. The page lists everyone who works in the portal, and says each person has exactly one role. There are four. The admin can do everything, including the team and the settings. The accountant views and exports, and handles disputes. The operator views, exports, makes payment links, gives refunds and handles disputes. The observer only views. Malika clicks "Invite a user", types her helper's email and name, and sees the four roles, each with a short line on what it can do. She chooses Operator and sends it. The page says the invitation was sent. Nobody ever types another person's password. In the helper's inbox, the email has a link. The helper sets their own password, and scans a code with an authenticator app on their phone. That second step is there because an operator can give money back. Then they sign in. The menu shows Payments, and other pages for the work. But there is no Users item at all. The operator cannot manage the team, so the screen does not offer it. Every action in the portal names the roles that may run it, and nothing more. That is least privilege: each person gets only what their job needs. So a stolen operator login cannot add new users or change settings. For her accountant, Malika picks the Accountant role: changing a role is one choice in a list. A role change takes effect at once, and the person is signed out. On the ninth of March, the rush is over. Malika clicks Block on her helper. A window asks first: they are signed out at once and can no longer sign in, and their past actions stay in the history. Every refund the helper gave still names the helper. Then, out of curiosity, Malika tries to block herself. She is the only admin. The window asks her to choose a new admin first, and the Block button stays grey until she does. A merchant can never lose its last admin, so nobody is ever locked out of their own shop. A login each means every refund and every export names a real person. And a person who leaves loses access the same minute. Next: Malika's second website.
