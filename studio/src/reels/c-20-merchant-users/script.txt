Should everyone at a merchant see and do everything? Picture a shop where the owner, the accountant and the support desk share one portal login. The support agent sends a refund to a friend, and the record says only that the shop did it. When the accountant leaves, the password must change for everyone. In a real shop, the owner, the accountant and the cashier each have their own badge. Each badge opens only the doors that job needs. A merchant's team works the same way. Here is how it works. Each person has their own login and exactly one of four merchant roles. The admin can do everything, including the team and the settings. The accountant views and exports, and handles disputes. The operator views, exports, makes payment links, gives refunds and handles disputes. The observer only views. An admin invites people by email. Nobody ever types another person's password. Each person sets their own password, and an authenticator app on their phone, because some of these people can give money back. Every action in the portal names the roles that may run it, and nothing more. That is least privilege: each person gets only what their job needs. Changing someone's role takes effect at once, and they are signed out. Blocking someone signs them out at once too. Their past actions stay in the history. And a merchant can never lose its last admin. The last admin cannot step down or be blocked until a new admin is named. So nobody is ever locked out of their own shop. Now watch the product. The viewer signs in as the merchant's admin and opens Users. The page says it lists everyone who works in the portal, and that each person has exactly one role. The viewer clicks "Invite a user", types an email and a name, and sees the four roles, each with a short line on what it can do. They choose Operator and send it. The page says the invitation was sent. Now the new person's inbox. The email has a link. They open it, set their own password, and scan a code with an authenticator app on their phone. Then they sign in. Their menu shows Payments, and other pages for their work. But there is no Users item at all. The operator cannot manage the team, so the screen does not offer it. Back as the admin. Changing a role is one choice in a list. Then the viewer clicks Block on a person. A window asks first: they are signed out at once and can no longer sign in, and their past actions stay in the history. Now the admin tries to block themself while they are the only admin. The window asks them to choose a new admin, and the Block button stays grey until they do. What does this protect? A shared login hides who did what. With a badge each, every refund and every export names a real person. Least privilege means a stolen operator login cannot add new users or change settings. Blocking at once means a person who leaves loses access the same minute. And the last-admin rule protects the merchant from locking itself out. So, like staff badges: everyone their own, and only the doors their job needs. Next: the merchant's websites.
