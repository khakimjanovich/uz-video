How do you tell the world about a payment without ever losing the news, or inventing it? In lesson twenty-seven, a payment ended paid or declined, and in lesson twenty-eight, even a silent one found its end. In lesson one, the shop's server was told how its payment ended. This lesson makes sure that news is never lost, and never invented. Picture an app that saves a payment as paid, and then tells the shop. If it crashes between those two steps, the shop never hears. The shop ships nothing, and the buyer has paid for nothing. Swap the order, and it is worse. The app tells the shop paid, then crashes before saving. Now the shop ships goods for a payment that does not exist. Two separate steps can always be split by a crash. Think of an office with an outbox tray. You close the file and drop the letter in the tray in one motion. Later, a postman collects from the tray. If you are called away, the letter is either in the tray or the file is still open. Nothing is half done. The payments product works the same way. When a payment reaches its end, such as paid or declined, its news is written to the outbox. The outbox is a tray inside the bank's own records. The news and the payment's final state are saved together, in one step. Either both are saved, or neither is. So there is never a paid payment without its news, and never news without a paid payment. Next comes the relay. The relay is the postman. It keeps collecting the oldest unsent news from the tray, and passes it to the bank's message stream. Several copies of the app may run at once. Each copy takes different letters, so no two pick the same one. Every piece of news is labelled with its payment. So all the news about one payment travels in one line, always in order. Paid can never overtake created. The relay marks a letter sent only after the stream confirms it has it. So consider a crash at the worst moment: after sending, but before marking. When the app starts again, it sends that letter once more. That is at-least-once delivery. A letter may arrive twice, but never zero times. To make repeats harmless, every piece of news carries its own id. A reader who sees an id twice simply drops the second one. One more rule keeps this simple. The bank's records stay the truth. The stream only carries the news about it. Now watch the visible behaviour. A buyer pays. In the merchant portal, the payment shows paid. Open it, and the webhooks section soon shows its first piece of news, on its way to the merchant's server. Now stop the bank's app at the exact moment a payment is paid. Start it again a minute later. The payment is still paid, and its news still goes out. Nothing is missing. On the merchant's server, a piece of news may now arrive twice. Both copies carry the same id. The merchant keeps the first and drops the second. What does this protect? It protects the merchant from waiting forever for news that was lost in a crash. It protects the merchant from shipping goods because of news about a payment that never happened. And it protects the order of events, so a merchant never sees refunded before paid. The buyer gets their goods because the shop always hears. The bank never has to explain news that its own records do not show. So back to the tray. Decide and post in one motion. The postman may knock twice, but never zero times. Next: delivering the news to the merchant's server.
