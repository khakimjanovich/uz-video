How much does the bank earn from each payment, and who decides it? The simplest way is one fixed fee for every store. But stores differ, and each one needs its own rate, sometimes per sales point or per card network. So the bank keeps commission rules: a percent, a fixed part, and an optional minimum and maximum. A rule can cover one sales point, a whole store or a card network, and it applies only within its configured period. Every new rule or change goes through four-eyes before it takes effect. When the buyer enters the card, the bank picks the most specific matching rule and calculates the fee. The fee is saved with the payment, so a later rule change never touches it. At settlement, the bank keeps that fee and pays the store the rest.
