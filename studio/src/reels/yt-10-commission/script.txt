How much does the bank earn on each payment, and who decides it? Fees can't be hard-coded, because every merchant negotiates its own. So the commission module keeps fee rules, each for a website, a merchant, a card scheme, or the whole bank. When several rules match, the most specific one wins: a website beats a merchant, which beats a card scheme, which beats the bank. The fee is a percent, rounded once to whole tiyin, plus a fixed part, kept between a minimum and a maximum. A fee that would take the whole amount is refused. Rules are never edited: a change adds a rule or ends one, and a second admin must approve it. Two active rules for the same scope can never apply at the same moment, and the database itself refuses them. To change a fee, the old rule ends at a chosen moment and the new one starts there. Other modules ask one question, what is the fee for this payment, and the payment keeps the rule and the amount. The module has its own schema and database user, built by a new shared persistence library. Unit tests cover the formula and the scope order, and integration tests run on a real Postgres. Under load, more than thirty thousand fee quotes came back in under three milliseconds. Next, the terminal each payment goes through.
