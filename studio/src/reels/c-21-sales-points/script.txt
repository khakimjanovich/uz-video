In April, Malika starts a second website, just for wedding flowers. Her company is approved, so she expects it to take payments the moment it goes up. It does not. In lesson eighteen, her company was switched on, and in lesson twenty, her team got their roles, and this lesson checks and switches on each website on its own, because each one is a different risk. If every website of an approved company could take payments the moment it was typed in, that company could add a copy of a famous brand's shop. Then all the bank's checks on the company would mean nothing. Malika opens Websites. The page says each website that takes payments is checked and switched on by the bank. She clicks Add website. She gives it a name, the website's address, and return addresses on that same site, pressing Enter after each one. Each website is a sales point: one place where buyers pay. When a payment ends, the buyer is sent back to the shop, and where they go is the return address. Every return address must be secure, and on the website's own site. I think this is the most underrated field in the whole form. Without that rule, a fraudster could use the bank's own payment page to send buyers to a fake thank-you page. Bekzod tests it: he tries a return address on somebody else's site. The server refuses it, and the screen shows why. Malika saves, and the page says the bank will check it. The new site starts as a draft, and a draft cannot take money. Return addresses can change only while the website is still a draft. At the bank, Dilnoza opens Malika's shop and the new website. A card at the top says "Before it takes payments", with a list of steps and how many are left. The Switch on button is grey. A line explains: switching on needs an active merchant, a passed check and chosen cards. Dilnoza looks at the site, clicks Record a check, chooses Passed, and saves. A failed check must say what is wrong. Then Deal terms. The deal says which cards the website takes, and it can say how much it may take per payment, and per day. She ticks one card. The boxes "Most per payment" and "Most per day" can stay empty, which means no limit. Three steps now show done, and the Switch on button lights up. One click. A warning appears: some cards still have no terminal or fee, and their payments are refused until they do. So Switch on is not the end. Each card still needs its own terminal and its own fee before its payments go through. And before taking any money, every part of the system asks one question: may this website take a payment right now? The answer is yes only when the website and its merchant are both active. Suspend either one, and payments stop at once, everywhere. The bank checked Malika's company once, and it still checks every website she opens. Next: how the bank's fee is decided.
