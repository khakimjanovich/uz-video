When a payment is slow or stuck, how would anyone know? One payment crosses the app, an adapter and a scheme, and the logs on each machine can't follow it. So the app and both adapters import one platform library, observability, and adding the jar is all a service does. It traces every request with Micrometer Tracing and OpenTelemetry, and carries the trace on every call: to the adapters, through Kafka, and into Temporal. One trace follows a payment from the payment page, through the app's modules, into the Uzcard adapter and out to the scheme. Every response names its trace in an X-Request-Id header, a 401 or 403 too, so a caller can quote it to support. The logs carry the same trace id, so a warning links straight to the trace it belongs to. Every log line is masked on every sink, every outgoing call too: card numbers keep their first six and last four digits, tokens their last four, and secrets are hidden. Spring Boot exports JVM, HTTP and database pool metrics, and the app adds only what Boot can't know: how payments end. Every debit is counted by card scheme and outcome: approved, declined, unknown, or failed before any money moved. And the scheme's answer time is measured, with fixed buckets that reach past its forty second limit. Health indicators check the file store, Vault, Keycloak and both adapters, and each one is also a gauge: one while it is up. Everything goes over OTLP to one stack: Prometheus for metrics, Tempo for traces, Loki for logs, and Grafana on top. The payments dashboard puts it together: debits by outcome, unknown debits in red, answer times, errors per service, and pool waits. Grafana's alert rules live as code in the infrastructure repository, and every alert is an email to the on-call list, Mailpit locally. When the scheme goes silent, the debit shows up as unknown, the adapter's client error fires an alert email, and the warning links to its trace.
