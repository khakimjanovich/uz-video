Sunday, twenty past nine. The buyer at the bus stop presses Confirm, and the screen freezes for a second. So the buyer presses again. Two presses, one bunch of tulips, and one card that must be charged exactly once. In lesson twenty-six, the page took a token from the card frame and ecom checked that it belongs to this payment, and the fee from lesson twenty-two and the terminal from lesson twenty-three are ready too. This lesson charges the card, exactly once. A double press is only the start. The page can retry on a weak network. Two copies of the bank's app can pick up the same confirmed card. Each of them could charge the card. And there is a worse case. The app asks the network to charge the card, and crashes before it saves the answer. Did money move? Nobody knows. Here is my rule for any code that moves money. Decide who goes, write it down, and only then act. First comes the claim. The claim is a single change to the payment, from card confirmed to debiting. It succeeds only if the payment is still card confirmed. So however many presses or retries arrive, exactly one caller wins. The others find the payment already taken, and stop. The winner then looks for two things. It finds the website's live terminal for this card. And it finds the fee the bank agreed with Malika. If either is missing, the payment fails right there, and nothing is charged. Next, the winner writes a line in the operation journal. That is the bank's logbook of every call that moves money. The line says debit, requested, for this payment, through this terminal. It is written before any network is asked. And a payment can hold only one debit line. Then ecom asks the card zone to charge the card by its token. The call carries its own id. If it is sent twice, the card zone sees the same id, and the charge still happens once. Timur's card zone talks to the card processing centre, which asks the buyer's bank. Some networks only hold the money at first. For those, the card zone takes the held money at once. That second step is the capture. So for every network, approved means the money is taken. Three answers can come back. Approved means paid, with a reference number. Declined is final, with a reason the bank can explain. And no answer in time is unknown. Unknown is never treated as declined. A paid payment never becomes unpaid again. Back at the bus stop, the page changes to taking the payment, and says the buyer's bank is being asked. A few seconds later, it says paid, and shows the reference number. Another buyer tries a card with too little money. The page says the payment was declined, and gives the reason: there isn't enough money on the card. That evening, Malika opens the merchant portal. The payments list shows the tulips as paid. She opens the payment. The details show the amount, the website and the bank fee. Below them is a section called calls to the card scheme. It has one line: debit, first sent, now approved. Two presses at the bus stop, and still only one line. Take away any step, and something breaks. Without the claim, a double press is a double charge. Without the journal written first, a crash leaves money moved and no record of it. Without the call's own id, a retried call charges the card again. And without unknown, a slow network would tell the buyer to pay again, while the first charge quietly went through. The buyer is charged once. Malika is paid once. And the bank can always say what it asked, and when. Next: what happens when the network goes silent.
