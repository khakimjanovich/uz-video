How do you make sure a card is charged exactly once? In lesson twenty-six, the page took a token from the card frame, and ecom checked it belongs to this payment. The fee from lesson twenty-two and the terminal from lesson twenty-three are ready too. This lesson charges the card, exactly once. Picture the buyer pressing pay twice. Or the page retrying on a weak network. Or two copies of the bank's app picking up the same confirmed card. Each of them could charge the card. Now picture a worse case. The app asks the network to charge the card, and crashes before it saves the answer. Did money move? Nobody knows. Think of a bank's cash desk. Only one cashier can hold the withdrawal slip. And the cashier writes the withdrawal in the logbook before opening the drawer. The charge works the same way. First comes the claim. The claim is a single change to the payment, from card confirmed to debiting. It succeeds only if the payment is still card confirmed. So however many clicks or retries arrive, exactly one caller wins. The others find the payment already taken, and stop. The winner then finds two things. It finds the website's live terminal for this card. And it finds the fee the bank agreed with this merchant. If either is missing, the payment fails right there, and nothing is charged. Next, the winner writes a line in the operation journal. That is the bank's logbook of every call that moves money. The line says debit, requested, for this payment, through this terminal. It is written before any network is asked. And a payment can hold only one debit line. Then ecom asks the card zone to charge the card by its token. The call carries its own id. If the call is sent twice, the card zone sees the same id, and the charge still happens once. The card zone talks to the processing centre, which asks the buyer's bank. Some networks only hold the money at first. For those, the card zone takes the held money at once. That second step is the capture. So for every network, approved means the money is taken. Three answers can come back. Approved means paid, with a reference number. Declined is final, with a reason the bank can explain. And no answer in time is unknown. Unknown is never treated as declined. A paid payment never becomes unpaid again. Now watch it from the buyer's side. The buyer types the card into the frame and confirms it there. The page changes to taking the payment, and says the buyer's bank is being asked. A few seconds later, it says paid, and shows the reference number. Try a card with too little money. The page says the payment was declined, and gives the reason: there isn't enough money on the card. Now open the merchant portal. The payments list shows the payment as paid. Open it. The details show the amount, the website and the bank fee. Below them is a section called calls to the card scheme. It has one line: debit, first sent, now approved. Even after a double click, there is still only one line. What does this protect? Without the claim, a double click is a double charge. Without the journal written first, a crash leaves money moved and no record of it. Without the once-only id, a retried call charges the card again. And without the unknown outcome, a slow network would tell the buyer to pay again, while the first charge quietly went through. The buyer is charged once. The merchant is paid once. And the bank can always say what it asked, and when. So back to the cash desk. One slip, in one hand, written in the logbook first. One charge. Next: what happens when the network goes silent.
