Before we write a single line of code, what actually happens when someone pays a shop online with a card? Five players are involved: the buyer, the shop, the acquirer bank, the processing center, and the issuer bank. The acquirer is the shop's bank, the one we are building, and the issuer is the bank that gave the buyer their card. Every shop gets a terminal at the acquirer, identified by a merchant ID and a terminal ID, the MID and TID. When the buyer pays, the acquirer sends an authorization through the processing center to the issuer. The issuer checks the balance and places a hold, so the money is reserved but not yet moved. Then the acquirer captures the hold, and the payment becomes final. A reversal cancels a payment before settlement, and a refund sends money back after it. At the end of the day, the processing center settles all payments with the acquirer. The acquirer keeps its commission and pays the shop the rest by the next banking day. These words, authorization, capture, MID, TID, and settlement, will name the classes we build in every episode that follows.
