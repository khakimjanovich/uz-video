Before any code, what actually happens when someone pays a shop online with a card? Five players are involved: the buyer, the shop, the acquirer bank, the processing center, and the issuer bank. The acquirer is the shop's bank, the one this series describes, and the issuer is the bank that gave the buyer their card. Every shop's website gets an e POS terminal for each card scheme, a virtual POS terminal identified by a merchant ID and a terminal ID, the MID and TID. When the buyer pays, the acquirer sends an authorization through the processing center to the issuer. Depending on the card scheme, the issuer either debits the money in a single step, or first places a hold on it. A hold is then captured, and only then is the payment final; every scheme, Uzcard and Humo today, Visa or Mastercard later, follows one of these two ways. A reversal cancels a payment before settlement, and a refund sends money back after it. At the end of the day, the processing center settles all payments with the acquirer. The acquirer keeps its commission and pays the shop the rest by the next banking day. These words, authorization, capture, MID, TID, and settlement, name the classes in every episode that follows.
