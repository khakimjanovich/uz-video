How do you follow one payment through the whole system? A merchant calls the bank. My customer's payment has been spinning for a minute, they say. That payment crossed the payment page, ecom, the card zone, an adapter and the processing centre. It touched four machines, and each machine writes its own log lines. Without one number to follow, someone searches four logs and guesses. Worse, the lines that would help most are the ones most likely to hold a card number. Think of a hospital. When you arrive, you get a wristband with one number. That number is on every chart, in every department: the lab, the scan, the ward. Anyone can follow your whole stay by that one number. Here, every request gets the same kind of number. We call it a trace: one number that follows a request from the moment it arrives until its answer leaves. When ecom calls the card zone, the trace goes along. When the card zone calls an adapter, it goes along again. So one request draws one line across both products. Every answer names its trace too, even an answer that says no. A merchant's server can quote that number to support, and support finds the whole story in seconds. Every log line carries the same number. So a warning in one log leads straight to the request that caused it, and to every other line about that request. Now the card numbers. Before any line is written, it is cleaned. This is log masking. A card number keeps only its first six and its last four digits; the middle becomes stars. Any long run of digits that could be a card number is treated as one. A token keeps only its last four characters. Passwords and secrets are hidden completely. This happens on every line, wherever the line is sent. So nothing the running system writes can leak a card. Each product sends all of this to its own monitoring. ecom has its own, and the card zone runs its own, separately. They share nothing here either, just as on the map. Let's watch the product. A support person opens ecom's monitoring. The first dashboard shows payments by how they end: paid, declined, and unknown. Unknown is painted red: payments where nobody yet knows whether money moved. Right now, the red line sits at zero. They paste the trace number the merchant quoted. One line appears, crossing ecom, the card zone and the adapter, with the time each step took. They open the log lines of that request. The card number shows its first six digits, a row of stars, and its last four. Now the processing centre goes quiet for a while. Payments start to stay unknown, and the red line climbs. Nobody is watching the screen. That does not matter, because of alert rules. An alert rule is a condition the system checks on its own, all the time, written down like the rest of the product. When payments stay unknown for too long, the rule fires. An email arrives: payments are stuck. It says what is wrong and when it started. Support opens the dashboard from the email and sees the same red line. What does this protect? Without the trace, a merchant's simple question costs an hour of guessing. Without masking, the logs become a second place where card numbers live, and the locked room has a hole in its wall. Without alerts, a stuck payment is found by an angry buyer, not by the bank. The trace protects the merchant's time. Masking protects the buyer's card. Alerts make sure the bank is the first to know, not the last. Back to the hospital. One number on every chart, in every department, and no secrets written on the wristband.
