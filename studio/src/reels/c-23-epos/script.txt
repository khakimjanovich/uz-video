Wednesday, three days before Malika's wedding site opens. Dilnoza has one step left on its card, and it is the one the processing centre cares about. In lesson twenty-one, the wedding site was switched on for one card, and in lesson eight, Timur's card zone spoke to each processing centre through its own adapter, and this lesson gives each website and card the terminal those centres know it by. In a shop, you pay at a card machine on the counter. Online there is no counter, but the processing centre still wants to know which machine sent each charge. It accepts charges only from machines it has issued. Send a charge with the wrong numbers, and the money is refused, or worse, booked to another merchant. So each online payment goes through a virtual card machine: an e-POS terminal. Its identity is a pair of numbers that the processing centre issues: a merchant number and a terminal number. On screen they are called the MID and the TID. Dilnoza opens the website's page. There is a section called e-POS terminals, with a hint: each card needs one live terminal from the processing centre, or its payments are refused. On a website with no cards chosen yet, the "Register e-POS terminal" button is grey. Malika's site already takes one card, so the button is live. Dilnoza clicks it. A form asks for the MID, the TID and the card. She types the pair the centre issued, and chooses the card. A bank admin registers each pair for one website and one card. There is only one live terminal per website and card, so each payment has exactly one way out. Where the processing centre requires it, the terminal is registered with the centre too, through Timur's card zone. It is kept only if the centre accepts it. If the centre refuses, or does not answer, nothing is saved. Here it accepts. The page says the terminal is registered. It appears in the list as Live, with who registered it. Next to it are three buttons: Suspend, Resume and Revoke. Suspend stops its payments on the bank's side alone, and it can be resumed. Revoking is final. Dilnoza opens Revoke, just to read it. The window warns that a revoked pair never comes back, and asks why, for the record. She closes it without revoking. Every change is written down: who, what, and why. A pair is never registered twice, not even after its terminal is closed. Think of a licence plate: issued once, one per car, never reused. Teams are tempted to reuse an old pair to save a call to the centre. I think that is the most expensive shortcut in this module, because a pair used twice mixes two merchants' histories forever. Now the card at the top, "Before it takes payments". The terminal step is ticked: every card has a live e-POS terminal. With the fee from the last lesson, every step is done. The card turns green and says "Ready: taking payments". And before every charge, the payment asks for the live terminal of its website and card. No terminal, no charge, and no money moves.
