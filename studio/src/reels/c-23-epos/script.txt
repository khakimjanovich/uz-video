Through which machine does an online payment go? In lesson twenty-one, each website was switched on for the cards it accepts. In lesson eight, the card zone spoke to each processing centre through its own adapter. This lesson gives each website and card the terminal those centres know it by. In a shop, you pay at a card machine on the counter. Online there is no counter, but the processing centre still wants to know which machine sent each charge. It accepts charges only from machines it has issued. Send a charge with the wrong numbers, and the money is refused, or worse, booked to another merchant. A taxi cannot take fares without a licence plate. A plate is issued once, one per car, and never reused, even after the car is scrapped. Here is how it works. Each online payment goes through a virtual card machine: an e-POS terminal. Its plate is a pair of numbers that the processing centre issues: a merchant number and a terminal number. On screen they are called the MID and the TID. A bank admin registers the pair for one website and one card. There is only one live terminal per website and card. And a pair is never registered twice, not even after its terminal is closed. Where a processing centre requires it, the terminal is registered with the centre too. It is kept only if the centre accepts it. If the centre refuses, or does not answer, nothing is saved. An admin can suspend a terminal. That stops its payments on the bank's side alone, and it can be resumed. Revoking is final. Every change is written down: who, what, and why. And before every charge, the payment asks for the live terminal of its website and card. No terminal, no charge, and no money moves. Now watch the product. The viewer is a bank admin on the website's page. There is a section called e-POS terminals, with a hint: each card needs one live terminal from the processing centre, or its payments are refused. On a website with no cards chosen yet, the "Register e-POS terminal" button is grey. Here the website already takes one card, so the button is live. The viewer clicks it. A form asks for the MID, the TID and the card. They type the pair the centre issued and choose the card. The page says the terminal is registered. It appears in the list as Live, with who registered it. Next to it are three buttons: Suspend, Resume and Revoke. The viewer opens Revoke, just to read it. The window warns that a revoked pair never comes back, and asks why, for the record. They close it without revoking. Now the card at the top, "Before it takes payments". The terminal step is ticked: every card has a live e-POS terminal. With the fee from the last lesson, every step is done. The card turns green and says "Ready: taking payments". What does this protect? A charge with the wrong pair can lose money, or book it to someone else. A pair used twice mixes two merchants' histories forever. One live terminal per website and card means each payment has exactly one way out. Asking for the live terminal before every charge means a suspended or missing terminal stops money before it moves. This protects the merchant's money, the buyer's charge, and the bank's books with the processing centre. So, like a taxi's licence plate: one per car, never reused, and no plate, no fares.
