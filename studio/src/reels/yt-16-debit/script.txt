What stops two clicks on the pay button from charging the card twice? Two requests to confirm the same payment can arrive at the same moment, and both would try to debit. One conditional update moves the payment from confirmed to debiting, and only one caller can win it. Anyone who loses the claim simply gets the payment as it stands. The winner fixes the route: the website's live e POS terminal for the card's scheme, with its MID and TID. It fixes the fee too: the most specific commission rule, the fee and the merchant's net, kept on the payment. With no e POS terminal or no fee rule, the payment fails right there, before any money moves. Every call to the scheme is written to an operation journal first, and the database allows only one debit per payment. Then the card module asks the scheme through its adapter, with our payment id as the reference, so a retry is never paid twice. An approval makes the payment paid, with the scheme's reference number, the RRN. A decline is final, with our own code for why. No answer leaves it unknown, never declined, because the money may already have moved. Every move goes through the status table, so a paid payment never becomes unpaid; it only moves on when its money goes back, which episode twenty-one explains. The buyer sees the result on the page, and the bank sees the fee, the e POS terminal and the RRN. Next, seeing inside the system when something is slow.
