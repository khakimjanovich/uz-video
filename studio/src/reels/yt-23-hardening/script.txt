A website is live and taking payments: what stops it from taking a fortune in one payment, or in one day? The bank sets two limits per website: the most one payment may be, and the most the website may take in one business day. Both are optional, and a daily limit below one payment's limit is refused, by the entity and by the database. One payment over its limit is refused when it is created, before a buyer ever sees a page. The daily limit is harder: five buyers pay at the same moment, and each of them alone would fit. So each debit reserves its amount with one statement: add it to the day only if the total stays within the limit. The row's lock makes racing debits wait their turn, and whatever doesn't fit gets nothing. The reservation commits together with the journal, before the scheme is asked: a debit that doesn't fit fails, and no money moves. A decline gives its share back, and so does a same-day reversal; an unknown debit keeps it, since the money may have moved. The day is the business day in the settlement time zone, the same day the reports add up. The bank edits both limits on the website's page, and the merchant sees them.
