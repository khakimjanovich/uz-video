Thursday, ten to eleven at night. A buyer pays Malika for a wedding bouquet, and ecom asks the card zone to charge the card. Then nothing. The card processing centre is slow, or its reply is lost on the way, and the bank's chosen waiting time runs out. The buyer's card may already be charged. Or it may not. In lesson twenty-seven, a charge like this was marked unknown, never declined, and in lesson eight, every charge carried our own payment id, so a centre can be asked about it later. This lesson asks that question, patiently, until the charge is found or a person decides. There are three tempting mistakes here. Call it declined, and the buyer pays again, so the card is charged twice. Send the charge again, and the result is the same. Wait in the app's memory, and the app restarts, and nobody ever finds out. I think silence is the hardest case in payments, because every quick answer to it is wrong. So the first rule is a refusal. An unknown charge is never declined, and never sent again, because the money may already have moved. The buyer's page tells them not to pay again. Then a durable workflow takes over. It is a task that remembers where it is, even when the app restarts. There is exactly one for each payment, so two copies can never chase the same charge. It asks the card processing centre about this one charge only, by the payment's own id. These are questions only. They never move money. Between questions it waits, and each wait is longer than the one before. That pattern is backoff. It gives a struggling network room to recover. Found means paid. Not found means declined, but only once the network has had time to know. If the charge is found after the buyer's page has closed, the order is already over. So the money goes back to the card. The questions stop before the card processing centre's daily cutoff, less the bank's chosen margin. That leaves time to give money back the same day. Anything still unknown then goes to manual review. A person at the bank checks with the network, and records the answer. Here is the wedding bouquet, as everyone sees it. On the buyer's phone, the page says we're confirming your payment, and adds: don't pay again. In Malika's merchant portal, the status reads checking with the bank. She opens it, and the calls to the card scheme show one debit line, with no answer. Most nights, the workflow gets its answer a little later. The status changes to paid, or to declined, and Malika is told. Tonight is the rare case. The day's deadline nears, and there is still no answer. The status changes to being checked by a person. In the bank's admin portal, Dilnoza sees the payment under needs review, with how long it has waited. She asks the network, then picks debited or not debited. For debited, she enters the network's reference for the charge. Either way, she writes a note: who at the network confirmed it, and how. That note is saved for audit. She presses resolve, and the payment takes its final state. By morning, the buyer has paid once, or not at all. Malika ships a bouquet only if it was paid, and never refuses one that was. The bank can explain every charge at the end of the day. And none of it depended on the app staying up, or on anyone remembering. Next: how the shop hears about a payment.
