What do you do when the network never answers? ecom has asked the card zone to charge a card. The processing centre is slow, or the reply is lost on the way. The bank's chosen waiting time runs out. The buyer's card may already be charged. Or it may not. There are three tempting mistakes. Call it declined, and the buyer pays again, so the card is charged twice. Send the charge again, and the result is the same. Wait in the app's memory, and the app restarts, and nobody ever finds out. Think of a parcel you sent. It left, but the tracking page says nothing. You do not post a second parcel. You check the tracking, and each time you wait a little longer before checking again. And before the post office closes, you go to the counter and ask a person. A silent charge works the same way. First, an unknown charge is never declined, and never sent again, because the money may already have moved. The buyer's page tells them not to pay again. Then a durable workflow takes over. It is a task that remembers where it is, even when the app restarts. There is exactly one for each payment, so two copies can never chase the same charge. It asks the processing centre about this one charge only, by the payment's own id. These are questions only. They never move money. Between questions it waits, and each wait is longer than the one before. That pattern is backoff. It gives a struggling network room to recover. Found means paid. Not found means declined, but only once the network has had time to know. If the charge is found after the buyer's page has closed, the order is already over. So the money goes back to the card. The questions stop before the processing centre's daily cut-off, less the bank's chosen margin. That leaves time to give money back the same day. Anything still unknown then goes to manual review. That means a person at the bank checks with the network, and records the answer. Now watch the product. On the buyer's side, the page says we're confirming your payment, and adds: don't pay again. In the merchant portal, the payment's status reads checking with the bank. Open it, and the calls to the card scheme show one debit line, with no answer. A little later, the workflow gets its answer. The status changes to paid, or to declined, and the merchant is told. Now the rare case. The day's deadline nears, and there is still no answer. The status changes to being checked by a person. In the bank's admin portal, the payment appears under needs review, with how long it has waited. An admin asks the network, then picks debited or not debited. For debited, the admin enters the network's reference for the charge. Either way, the admin writes a note: who at the network confirmed it, and how. That note is saved for audit. The admin presses resolve, and the payment takes its final state. What does this protect? It protects the buyer from paying twice for one order. It protects the merchant from shipping goods that were never paid, or refusing an order that was. It protects the bank from a charge nobody can explain at the end of the day. And it does not depend on the app staying up, or on anyone remembering. So back to the parcel.
