How do you stop someone testing stolen cards on a payment page? Picture a thief with a bag of keys, trying key after key in a shop's lock. Locking the door after three wrong keys does not help: they walk to the next door. Here the keys are stolen card numbers, and the doors are payment sessions. A thief with ten thousand stolen numbers tries them on a small shop's page, to find the ones that still work. That is card testing. Every try reaches a card network, and may text a code to a real person's phone. So the card zone counts every attempt before any network is asked: per card, by its fingerprint, per first digits, per buyer's address, and per shop. A velocity limit allows only so many failed tries within a time window. Past the limit, a new try is refused before any network is asked. Only failures that say something about the card count. And when refusals pile up, an alert goes out to the team. Count the keys, not the doors. Next: how a merchant signs up.
