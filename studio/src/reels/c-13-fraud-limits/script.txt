How do you stop someone testing stolen cards on your payment page? Picture a thief with a bag of keys. They try key after key in a shop's lock, to find the ones that fit. Locking the door after three wrong keys does not help: they just walk to the next door. Here the keys are stolen card numbers, and the doors are payment sessions. A thief with ten thousand stolen numbers tries them on a small shop's payment page, to find the ones that still work. That is card testing. Every try reaches a card network, and it may text a code to a real person's phone. Each session allows only a few tries, as lesson eleven showed. But the thief just opens a new session. So the card zone writes down every attempt before any network is asked. It keeps the attempt by the card's fingerprint and its first digits, never by its number. And it notes the payer's address and the shop. That gives four counters: the card, the first digits, the address, and the shop. A velocity limit allows only so many attempts within a time window. Past the limit, for any one of the four counters, a new try is refused before any network is asked. Only refusals that say something about the card count against it, like a wrong code. Our own failures never count, like a card network that is down. Each refusal is counted, and an alert email goes out to the team. Now read the rules in the card zone's code. The note at the top says what is counted: per card, per first digits, per payer address, and per shop. Past the limit, a new try is refused before any card network is asked. Further down, one short list names what is never counted: our own state, and our own unavailability. And a new session for a shop past its limit is refused before it opens.
