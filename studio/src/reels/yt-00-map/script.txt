What does it take to build a bank's e-commerce acquiring system from scratch? In this series we build one, part by part, and this map is where we track every step. On top are the three users: the merchant's server, the buyer, and the bank admin. Inside is one modular monolith with five modules. At the edges, two ports talk to the outside world: email, and the processing center. Below sits the shared infrastructure: Postgres, Redis, Keycloak, Vault, and Temporal. Part zero brings that infrastructure up with a single command. Part one builds the merchants: sign-up, email codes, two-admin approval, and API credentials. Part two adds the money rules: the commission, and the terminals we debit through. Part three handles cards: a fake processing center, the adapters, and a vault that stores only tokens. Part four builds the payment itself, and it comes last because it needs everything before it. Part five moves the card vault into its own service, to show why clean boundaries matter. Next, we set up the project and bring the whole world up with one command.
