What does it take to build a bank's e-commerce acquiring system from scratch? This series walks through one, part by part, and this map shows where each part sits. On top are the three users: the merchant's server, the buyer, and the bank admin. The system is two units, and the first, ecom, is the payments product: one modular monolith with five modules. Partner owns the merchants: who they are, their company documents, and whether they may sell. Commission decides the fee on every payment. The e POS module knows which e POS terminal, a MID and a TID, each payment goes through. Payment creates every payment, debits the card by its token, and resolves the outcome. Reconciliation compares every day's debits, reversals and refunds with what each card processing centre reports, and gives each one a match state and a reason. ecom never sees a card number: everything that touches one lives in the second unit, the cards unit. The buyer types the card into the card frame, which sits inside the payment page. The card vault keeps card numbers encrypted, and hands out tokens instead. Its BIN module knows which scheme and which bank issued a card, from its first digits. One adapter per card processing centre speaks that centre's protocol, for the card vault and for ecom, over mutual T L S. Each unit has its own infrastructure: ecom runs Postgres, Redis, Keycloak, Vault, an S3 file store, Temporal, Kafka, and monitoring with Prometheus and Grafana, and the cards unit runs its own Postgres, Vault and Keycloak. Part zero lays the foundations: what acquiring is, the project setup, and how a domain becomes a contract and a database. Part one covers the merchants: sign-up, email codes, company documents, two-admin approval, Keycloak clients, merchant users, sales points, and the shared platform libraries. Part two holds the money rules: the commission, and the e POS terminals every debit goes through. Part three handles cards: the processing centre adapters, then the card vault with its BIN module. Part four is the payment, last, because it needs everything else: from signed requests and the payment page to refunds, daily reports, disputes and reconciliation. Part five is how it grows up: delivery onto a bank's own servers, and the cards unit up close. Next, what acquiring is, in five minutes.
