What does it take to build a bank's e-commerce acquiring system from scratch? In this series we build one, part by part, and this map is where we track every step. On top are the three users: the merchant's server, the buyer, and the bank admin. Inside is one modular monolith with five modules. Partner owns the merchants: who they are, and whether they may sell. Commission decides the fee on every payment. Terminal knows which bank terminal, the MID and TID, each payment goes through. The card vault keeps card numbers encrypted, and hands out tokens instead. Payment creates every payment, debits the card, and resolves the outcome. At the edges, two ports talk to the outside world: email, and the processing center. Below sits the shared infrastructure: Postgres, Redis, Keycloak, Vault, Temporal, Kafka, and monitoring with Prometheus and Grafana. Part zero brings up Postgres, Redis, and Keycloak with a single command, and the rest arrives when a problem needs it. Part one builds the merchants: sign-up, email codes, two-admin approval, and API credentials kept in Vault. Part two adds the money rules: the commission, and the terminals we debit through. Part three handles cards: the Uzcard and Humo adapters, and a vault that stores only tokens. Part four builds the payment last, with monitoring, retries, and Kafka events, because it needs everything else. Part five moves the card vault into its own service, to show why clean boundaries matter. Next, we set up the project and bring the whole world up with one command.
