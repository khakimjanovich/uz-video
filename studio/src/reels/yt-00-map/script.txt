What does it take to build a bank's e-commerce acquiring system from scratch? This series walks through one, part by part, and this map shows where each part sits. On top are the three users: the merchant's server, the buyer, and the bank admin. Inside is one modular monolith with six modules. Partner owns the merchants: who they are, their company documents, and whether they may sell. Commission decides the fee on every payment. Terminal knows which bank terminal, the MID and TID, each payment goes through. The BIN module knows which scheme and which bank issued a card, from its first digits. The card vault keeps card numbers encrypted, and hands out tokens instead. Payment creates every payment, debits the card, and resolves the outcome. Below the app, separate services do one job each: email, a file service for documents, and an adapter for each card scheme, Uzcard and Humo. The shared infrastructure is Postgres, Redis, Keycloak, Vault, an S3 file store, Temporal, Kafka, and monitoring with Prometheus and Grafana. Part zero brings up Postgres and Redis with a single command, and the rest arrives when a problem needs it. Part one builds the merchants: sign-up, email codes, company documents, two-admin approval, and logins and API clients in Keycloak. Part two adds the money rules: the commission, and the terminals every debit goes through. Part three handles cards: the Uzcard and Humo adapters as their own services, the BIN module with its bank directory, and a vault that stores only tokens. Part four builds the payment last, with monitoring, retries, and Kafka events, because it needs everything else. Part five deploys everything to Kubernetes, then moves the card vault into its own service, to show why clean boundaries matter. Next, the project setup, which brings the whole world up with one command.
