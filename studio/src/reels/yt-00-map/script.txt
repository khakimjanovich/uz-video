What does it take to build a bank's e-commerce acquiring system from scratch? In this series we build one, part by part, and this map is where we track every step. On top are the three users: the merchant's server, the buyer, and the bank admin. Inside is one modular monolith with five modules. Partner owns the merchants: who they are, and whether they may sell. Commission decides the fee on every payment. Terminal knows which bank terminal, the MID and TID, each payment goes through. The card vault keeps card numbers encrypted, and hands out tokens instead. Payment creates every payment, debits the card, and resolves the outcome. At the edges, two ports talk to the outside world: email, and the processing center. Below sits the shared infrastructure: Postgres, Redis, Keycloak, Vault, and Temporal. Part zero brings that infrastructure up with a single command. Part one builds the merchants: sign-up, email codes, two-admin approval, and API credentials. Part two adds the money rules: the commission, and the terminals we debit through. Part three handles cards: a fake processing center, the adapters, and a vault that stores only tokens. Part four builds the payment itself, and it comes last because it needs everything before it. Part five moves the card vault into its own service, to show why clean boundaries matter. Next, we set up the project and bring the whole world up with one command.
