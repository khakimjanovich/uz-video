Aziz still has a phone number that he has not used since university. He gave it to the state services portal years ago, and then he forgot it. MyID did not forget. His face passed MyID in the lift, so whose phone number is on his record? That old number now sits inside his verified identity, behind the one closed door of lesson fourteen. The money for his mother still waits, and one step stands between Aziz and level two: the phone check. Level two means two things at once. MyID proved the person, and the phone number of the account belongs to that person. A face proves who holds the phone. It does not prove that the number is his. Picture a man who gets a SIM card with a stranger's number, and passes MyID with his own face. Without a phone check, he would get level two on a number that belongs to somebody else. So, after Aziz confirms his data, the phone check starts. Step one: the platform sends TAF a face confirmation, with his personal identification number and his phone number. The platform sends it, so the app never sees the personal identification number. Step two: the platform compares his phone number with the number from MyID, as full international numbers. When they are the same, the status is Confirmed, and the platform asks TAF nothing more. But Aziz's numbers differ: the MyID one is his old university number. The next step also runs when MyID gives no number at all. Step three: the platform asks TAF one question: does this phone number belong to this personal identification number? TAF compares the number with the personal identification number from step one. Owner gives Confirmed. Not owner closes the record at level one. Account not found, or no answer from TAF, gives Processing. Today, TAF says owner. His status is Confirmed, and Aziz is at level two. The bank gets an event that his identity is verified, with no personal data in it. Now suppose that TAF had said: not owner. Then the number belongs to somebody else, and the platform closes his record at level one. It sends an SMS to the number that says the number is not his, and it counts a failed attempt for the number and the phone. Now suppose that TAF had given no answer. His status would be Processing, and he would stay at level one. The app shows him the word Processing. The platform opens a phone review, and sends an event to the bank's antifraud system. The review shows an operator his phone number, his personal identification number masked, the MyID number masked except the last four digits, the time, the device and the app. There is no timer: Aziz stays at level one until a person decides. Processing is an honest state: the platform admits that it does not know, and waits for a person instead of a guess. Operators get no message for each review; they watch the review list in the admin panel. When a review stays open longer than the configured age, the platform sends an alert to operations. The operator approves or rejects, and must write a reason. An approval starts only after a second operator agrees. Then Aziz gets level two, as with Confirmed. A rejection starts at once: it closes his record at level one, and he gets the SMS that the number is not his. Both decisions go to the audit trail. Back to the real Aziz, who is confirmed. His record keeps each check of his number, with the provider, the app and the time. A code through TAF, in the mobile bank, at his registration on Sunday of the first week. A MyID check today, which gave a different number. And the owner answer from TAF, a moment later. So the bank can always show how it knows that this number is his. Now a check for you. Suppose MyID had given the same number that Aziz has on his phone: does the platform still ask TAF who owns it? No: the same number gives Confirmed at once, and the platform asks TAF only when the numbers differ or MyID has no number. The phone check proves that the number belongs to the person. It compares the number from MyID first, and asks TAF only when that number is different or missing. Owner gives level two, not owner closes the record at level one, and no answer opens a phone review. An approval needs two operators, and a rejection needs one. Aziz is at level two, and the money for his mother is one step closer. Lesson sixteen shows how his phone becomes the one phone that can move money.
