Aziz still has a phone number that he has not used since university. He gave it to the government services portal years ago, and then he forgot it. MyID did not forget. So when his face passes MyID today, whose phone number is on the record? In lesson seventeen, the verified identity went into the protected class, with the phone number from MyID inside it. This lesson uses that number for one job: the phone check, the last step before level two. Why is a face not enough? Level two means two things at once: MyID proved the person, and the phone number of the account belongs to that person. A face proves who holds the phone. It does not prove that the number is his. Picture a man who gets a SIM card with a stranger's number, and passes MyID with his own face. Without a phone check, he would get level two on a number that belongs to somebody else. So, after Aziz confirms his data, the phone check starts. It runs only in a step-up, and a refresh does not repeat it. Step one: the platform sends TAF a face confirmation, with Aziz's personal identification number and his verified phone number. The app never sees the personal identification number, because the platform sends it. Step two: the platform compares Aziz's verified number with the number from MyID, as full international numbers. The same number gives the status Confirmed. But Aziz's numbers differ: the MyID one is his old university number. Step three: the platform asks TAF a second question: does this phone number belong to this personal identification number? TAF compares the number with the personal identification number from step one. Owner gives Confirmed. Not owner, account not found, or no answer from TAF gives Processing. Today, TAF says owner. Aziz's status is Confirmed, and three things happen together. He is at level two, in every app of the bank. His phone becomes a trusted device, and he gets a new access grant with level two. And linking starts, which the next lesson explains. Now suppose that TAF had said: not owner. Aziz's status would be Processing. He would stay at level one, and the app would show him the word Processing. The platform opens a phone review, and sends an event to the bank's antifraud system. The review shows an operator his phone number, his personal identification number masked, the MyID number masked except the last four digits, the time, the device and the app. There is no timer. Aziz stays at level one until a person decides. I think Processing is the bravest state in this design. The platform admits that it does not know, and it waits for a human instead of a guess. The operator approves or rejects, and must write a reason. Approval does what Confirmed did: level two, a trusted device, and linking. Rejection keeps Aziz at level one, with the status Rejected. Both send him a security notification, and both go to the audit trail. And the review list in the admin panel is the only alert that operators get. Back to the real Aziz, who is confirmed. His record now has a short history next to the number. Each check of the number is kept, with the provider, the app and the time. A code from the bank's SMS service, in the marketplace, last March. A TAF check, in the mobile bank, at registration. A MyID check, in the mobile bank, today. So the bank can always answer one question: how do we know that this number is Aziz's? To recap: a face confirmation to TAF, then the number from MyID, then TAF ownership, then a person; Confirmed gives level two, and Processing waits for a review. Aziz is confirmed, and the platform knows his personal identification number. But is he already a customer of the bank, with accounts and cards somewhere in its records? And how does the platform find him there, without ever trusting a phone number? Next: linking.
