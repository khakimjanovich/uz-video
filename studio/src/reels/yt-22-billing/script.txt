At the end of the day, a merchant asks one question: how much do I get? Every night the bank adds up the day before, for each website: what was paid, the fee, what was refunded, and the payout. A business day runs from midnight to midnight in the settlement time zone, the same day that decides reversal or refund, so the two never disagree. Paid counts payments paid that day and not reversed; refunded counts refunds made that day, whenever the payment was. The payout is the net of what was paid, less what was refunded: the bank keeps its fee on a refund. Building a day again replaces its rows, and never adds to them. So every instance may run the job, and each night it rebuilds the last three days: a night the app was down is caught up. On the scheme's side, Humo finalises an e POS terminal's payments, and pays its returns back, only after the terminal is reconciled. So the card module reconciles every Humo e POS terminal fifteen minutes before each clearing session, and once between eleven forty-five and midnight; a unique row per terminal and slot lets only the first instance run it. Behind each day is a CSV: every payment and refund, with its fee, card and RRN, and a refund as a negative line. Every cell is quoted, because a website's name may hold a comma. In the portal, the merchant sees each day and downloads its CSV; a day where refunds outweigh payments shows a negative payout. Next, hardening: limits on what one website may take.
