How many ways into the locked room should there be? The room has two kinds of visitor. Buyers' browsers come from the internet, to type a card into the frame. And services from other units, like ecom, come from their own servers. One big entrance that opened every visitor's traffic would see everything in clear text, and would be the most valuable box in the bank. So the room has two narrow doors, and nothing else listens. Think of a bank counter and a mail slot. The payers' door is the counter. It opens the browser's encrypted connection, passes on only the card frame's few requests, and answers not found to everything else. It also limits how fast one buyer can submit cards. The callers' door is the mail slot. A caller's connection arrives sealed, with the name of the service it wants written on the outside. The door reads only that name and passes the envelope on, still sealed: pass-through. Only a few names pass, and the service behind the door checks the caller itself. So no door ever holds every caller's traffic in clear text. Next: how a caller gets its certificate.
