What may you keep about a card? When your bank calls you, they say: your card ending seven eight nine zero. They never ask for the three digits on the back. Now picture a support engineer who pastes a whole card number into a ticket. The number now lives in the ticket's backups, its search, and with everyone who reads tickets. Systems should talk about cards the way your bank does. The long number on the front is the PAN, the card's own number. In this system it lives in one place only, the card zone. The code on the back must never be kept once a payment is approved, and this system never even asks for it. Screens and reports may show the masked number: the first six digits and the last four, with nothing between. Every other system keeps a card token: a random name the card zone gives the card. Nothing in it comes from the number, so a token tells a thief nothing. So the support ticket should have held the masked number or the token, never the PAN. Your bank never asks for the code on the back, and our systems never hold the PAN: they hold the masked number and the token. Next: where a card number may live.
