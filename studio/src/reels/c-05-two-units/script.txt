How can ecom take a card payment without ever seeing the card? Think of a real shop. When you pay, the cashier turns the card reader towards you. That reader belongs to the bank, not the shop. You type your PIN into the bank's machine, and the shop only gets a receipt. Now think of the obvious web design. A form on ecom's page sends the card number to ecom's server, and ecom passes it on. That one hop puts every card number through ecom. Then ecom's whole system would need the full check from the last lesson. So this system does what the real shop does: it puts the bank's card reader on ecom's counter. Here is how it works, step by step. First, ecom and the card zone never just talk freely. They agree on every question they may ask each other, and every answer, written down once. That agreement is a contract. ecom can only ask what the contract allows, and the card zone answers only in the shape the contract says. Second, when a buyer opens the payment page, ecom asks the card zone for a card-entry session. A card-entry session is a short-lived place to type one card, for one payment. The card zone answers with an address. Third, the payment page shows that address inside a frame. The frame is a small window inside the page, and it belongs to the card zone, not to ecom. That frame is the bank's card reader, standing on ecom's counter. Fourth, the buyer types the card into the frame. The card goes straight from the buyer's browser to the card zone. ecom's servers are not on the way. Fifth, the card zone locks the number away and hands the page a receipt: the card token, the masked number, and the buyer's bank. Never the number itself. Sixth, ecom asks the card zone to charge the card by its token. Before it does, ecom checks that this token really came from this payment's own session. Now watch the product. A buyer is sent from the shop to the bank's payment page. At the top it says "Secure payment page of the bank". It shows the order and the amount to pay. Below sits a box called "Card details": that is the frame, the bank's reader. The buyer types the card number and the date. The box hands back one short line: the masked number and the buyer's bank. The buyer presses Pay. The page says "Taking the payment", then "Your bank is being asked". A few seconds later it says "Paid", with a reference number. Now try it the wrong way. Leave the page open too long, or get the card wrong too many times. The frame says the attempt ended, too many tries or the time ran out, and asks for the card again. The session was short-lived on purpose. Each session binds one card only, and then it is used up. In the bank admin portal, this payment's row shows a masked number. That is all ecom ever holds: the token, the mask, the buyer's bank and the card network. What does this protect? The buyer's card never passes through ecom, so ecom's servers, logs and backups never hold a card number. A leak there leaks no card. The shop never touches the card either. And the bank keeps its full check on one small room, not on ecom's whole system. But the token still matters. It can start a payment, so ecom guards it like a ticket that can fetch a coat. That is why ecom keeps its smaller check. Back to the counter. The bank's reader faces the buyer. The shop keeps receipts in its drawer, never cards. Next: how every service gets its settings.
