Friday, eleven at night. A call reaches Timur's card zone. Its header says: I am ecom, charge this card. Anyone can type that line. A name you write yourself proves nothing. In lesson six, every key came from Vault, and a service with a missing setting refused to start. This lesson uses those keys to prove, on every call, that the caller really is ecom. Timur gets into the bank every morning the same way. He shows an ID card from the building's own pass office. Reception gives him a day pass that lists the floors he may visit, and the lift goes only to those floors. The card zone checks every call like that: two proofs, from two places, and a list of floors. The first proof is a certificate: a digital ID card, signed by the card zone's own pass office. When a caller connects, it shows its certificate. The card zone shows its own certificate back. So each side knows who is on the other end. Because both sides show one, this is called mutual TLS. If a caller has no certificate from the card zone's pass office, the connection is refused before any request is even read. The second proof is an access token: the day pass. The caller asks for it from the card zone's own login server, using its own secret. The card zone trusts only that login server. A pass from another product's login server is worth nothing here. Inside the access token is who it belongs to, and its roles. A role is a floor: one kind of operation. One role lets a caller bind a card. Another lets it read a card. Another lets it charge a card. Every operation needs its own role, and anything not listed is refused. So even the real ecom can only do what its roles allow. No caller at all holds the role for the card zone's admin operations. Many teams stop at the first proof. I think one proof is never enough: steal one file, and you are in. These checks have no screen, so read Timur's log for that Friday night. A caller with no certificate knocks on the partner door. The connection closes at once. No request is read, and nothing is answered. Next, a caller brings a valid certificate, but a pass from the wrong login server. The card zone answers: unauthorized. The pass did not come from its own login server, so it counts for nothing. Then the real ecom calls, with both proofs. It asks to charge a buyer's card by its token, for Malika's shop. Its pass carries the role to charge, so the call goes through. Then ecom asks for an admin operation, one that searches cards. Both proofs pass. But ecom's pass has no admin role, so the card zone answers: forbidden. Three refusals, for three different reasons. No certificate means no connection. A bad pass means unauthorized. A missing role means forbidden. Each answer tells Timur exactly which check stopped the call. The card zone keeps its own pass office and its own login server. Neither is shared with ecom, so nothing outside the room can hand out the room's passes. So steal only the certificate, and you have no pass. Steal only the pass, and you cannot even connect. Steal both from ecom, and you still reach only ecom's floors, never the admin floor. And a mistake inside ecom cannot give ecom more power, because only the card zone grants the roles. Saturday morning, Timur reads the log: three refusals and one charge, and each says exactly where it stopped. Next: how the card zone talks to every card network.
